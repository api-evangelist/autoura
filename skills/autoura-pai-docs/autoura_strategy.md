## Core thesis

Tourism is shifting from:

- selling access to experiences  
  to
- designing and operating experiences in real time

Autoura exists to enable this shift.

AI does not just help users choose what to do — it **runs the visit itself**.

### Curation as the core value

The core value of Autoura is **curation**.

Facts, listings, and content are widely available.

What is not widely available is:

- what matters
- how it is prioritised
- how it is sequenced into a coherent experience

Autoura structures and delivers this curation so AI systems can **operate real-world experiences effectively**.

## What we are doing and what we care about

### AI-native experiences

We believe the next baseline technologies for tourism are:

- AI agents (decision-making and coordination)
- AI wearables (e.g. AI glasses)
- autonomous mobility (e.g. self-driving vehicles)

Together, these enable fully personalised, real-time experiences to be designed and operated at scale — not just discovered, but created dynamically.

#### From discovery to design

AI wearables (such as AI glasses) and autonomous vehicles are not just interfaces — they are what enable AI agents to **design and operate itineraries in the real world at scale**.

Without these technologies:

- systems can only **discover and surface existing tours**
- experiences remain constrained to what has already been created and packaged

With them:

- AI agents can **design entirely new itineraries dynamically**
- coordinate movement, timing, and context in real time
- operate thousands of personalised journeys simultaneously

This is a fundamental shift:

- from **discovering pre-defined tours**
- to **designing and operating experiences on demand**

Autoura is built for this shift — enabling AI agents to move beyond selection into **designing and operating real-world experiences**.

To support this, tourism must move beyond booking-focused data.

Historically, tourism data has been designed for:

- search
- comparison
- booking

This is no longer sufficient.

To operate real-world experiences, systems need:

- structured, contextual, real-time data
- sequencing and pacing information
- in-visit guidance and decision support

Autoura focuses on structuring and distributing this data so AI systems can:

- guide visitors through experiences
- adapt in real time
- operate the visit itself, not just sell access to it

### AI as the experience layer

AI is not just tooling — it becomes the **experience layer itself**.

It:
- designs the visit
- adapts it in real time
- delivers guidance during the experience

This changes the product from:
- static content and bookings  
  to
- dynamic, real-world experience orchestration

### Beyond commercial tourism

The majority of tourism activity is **non-commercial or low-cost (typically inexpensive to access and not a major purchase decision)**.

Examples include:

- going to the beach
- walking around a city centre
- exploring public spaces
- informal discovery and wandering
- low-cost attractions

These experiences are typically:

- unstructured
- loosely organised
- not designed around or optimised through traditional booking systems

This is where AI has the greatest opportunity to transform the experience.

By providing:

- guidance
- sequencing
- context
- personalisation

AI can significantly improve experiences that were previously unsupported by traditional tourism systems.

Over time, this expands into more commercial tourism.

As AI becomes embedded in everyday, low-cost experiences, it builds familiarity, trust, and usage. This naturally extends into higher-value and more structured experiences, influencing how visitors plan, choose, and experience paid attractions.

- Attraction visits often have limited or no human interaction

### Evolution of day tour operators

AI does not remove day tour operators — it changes their role.

Day tour operators move from **operating entire linear experiences** to **curating high-impact moments within a broader, AI-orchestrated journey**.

The commercial pitch for tour operators is often **venue-first demand**, not replacement of their own tour catalogue.

Some visitors begin with a tour operator and choose a tour that includes a venue. Many others begin with the venue itself, such as Windsor Castle, Petra, or Buckingham Palace, and only then look for a guide, tour, transport, planning help, access support, or a better way to experience that place.

Autoura helps tour operators reach that second group. The venue visit creates the context where a relevant operator product can appear as the useful next action for someone already thinking about that venue.

This is not the pitch for the venue, and it is not the demo pitch. For a tour operator, the pitch is:

> People who are already thinking about a venue should discover your relevant tour or guide service at the moment they need help with that venue.

#### From third-person to first-person

Traditional day tours often present experiences in the third person:

- “we will visit a winery”
- “you will see how wine is made”

In AI-native experiences, this shifts to **first-person, direct interaction**:

- “here is how I make the wine”
- “this is my process”
- “taste this and notice…”

This creates:

- stronger human connection
- authenticity
- direct storytelling from the source

AI becomes the **connector and orchestrator**, not the storyteller in these **human, first-person moments**.

#### From long tours to modular moments

Traditional day tours are often:

- fixed
- long (e.g. 3–6 hours)
- linear

AI-native experiences break these into **modular moments**, for example:

- 40 minutes with a cheesemaker
- 1 hour at a vineyard
- 20 minutes exploring a village

These are:

- independently valuable
- dynamically sequenced
- adaptable to the visitor

AI provides the **glue between moments**, handling:

- sequencing
- timing
- transitions
- context

#### From operator to curator

The role of the day tour operator evolves into:

- curating high-quality, first-person experiences
- defining how and when those experiences can be accessed
- contributing expertise and narrative at key moments

Rather than controlling the full journey, they:

- contribute **high-impact components**
- integrate into a wider ecosystem of experiences

This enables:

- more flexible visitor journeys
- better use of local expertise
- more scalable and personalised experiences

### Experience quality

Experience quality is achieved through a hybrid model combining structured design and local expertise.

#### Visits (foundation)

Autoura is responsible for the **design and system-level curation of the visit**:

- ensuring structure, sequencing, and pacing
- maintaining consistency across the platform
- defining a usable experience where no operator input exists

- how time is spent at a place
- sequencing and flow within the venue
- pacing and prioritisation
- real-world constraints (layout, access, timing)

This ensures that each place has a coherent, consistent, and reusable representation.

#### Routes (alternative structure)

Routes provide an alternative way to structure experiences:

- a fixed, ordered sequence of stops across multiple places
- guiding movement between locations within a defined journey

Unlike visits:

- routes are not canonical representations of places
- multiple routes may exist for the same area or theme
- structure is defined at the journey level, not the place level

#### Role of contributors

Operators, attractions, and local contributors provide **expertise and enrichment**:

- first-person experiences and storytelling
- domain knowledge and craft
- access to experiences that cannot be replicated

These roles are distinct and complementary:

- contributors provide **high-quality moments**
- Autoura ensures those moments are structured into **coherent visits or routes**

#### System-level quality

Where contributor input is unavailable (e.g. public spaces, informal exploration), Autoura still defines structure to ensure the experience remains usable.

Quality is therefore systemic:

- strong, consistent structure at the visit level
- flexible and varied structure at the route level
- enriched by local expertise where available

### Positive impact

We aim to create positive outcomes for local tourism and local economies.

- work with local tour operators and tour guides
- augment local expertise rather than replace it
- keep tourism revenue and culture local

This is a **hybrid model** combining AI and local human expertise.
